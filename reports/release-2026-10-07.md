## Architecture and source

The Intelligent Contract owns adjudication and shared verdict state. The website supplies a claim and candidate URLs, manages signatures and fees, then verifies the contract result's owner, policy and exact input hash. A successful EVM receipt alone never becomes a verdict. Accepted results are provisional, and finalization without agreement remains no consensus.

## Limits established by testing

- Models and committees can disagree or time out even for a true, accessible claim. A passing GenVM replay does not guarantee committee agreement.
- Wikipedia statistics are candidates, not authoritative proof for every date or scope. Long tables can fall outside the 6,000-character page / 12,000-character total budget. Narrow record pages and matching counting rules matter.
- An X claim checked using independent evidence does not authenticate the X post, account, author or media. Direct X access remains variable.
- Misleading is implemented and covered by direct fixtures, but has no verified real committee example in this release matrix.
- The current revision's insufficient-evidence simulation passed, but its actual unrelated-source committee attempt did not produce an agreed result within the bounded window. The earlier revision's finalized Insufficient evidence result cannot substitute for current-revision proof.
- Source publication dates, the request reference date and the model's assumed clock must be distinguished. The reference-date fix reduces one observed error; it does not make undated evidence current.
- Consensus failure and an agreed Insufficient evidence verdict mean different things. Both can consume a network fee. The app does not automatically retry a payment.
- Private is a website access setting, not blockchain confidentiality. No confidential claim should be submitted.
- The stable SDK/ABI integration has measured EVM gas fees. It is not a completed Consensus v0.6 migration or a v0.6 fee profile.
